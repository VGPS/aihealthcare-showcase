## 2. Digital Health (80K+ members)

**Audience:** Startup founders, investors, product leaders, digital health strategists
**Angle:** The CONFIRMED vs. SPECULATIVE classification gap — how fast "reportedly" disappears
from headlines and why it matters for anyone using market news to make decisions

---

Three of last week's biggest AI-healthcare headlines — a $350M raise, a major pharma
partnership, and an acquisition — were still tagged SPECULATIVE in my system when they were
already being cited as confirmed fact across the industry.

That's not a criticism of the journalism. It's just what happens to information at speed:
"reportedly" disappears by the third republish, and a rumored deal reads like a closed one.

I built a classification system into my market digest specifically to resist this drift:
every headline is labeled CONFIRMED (second independent source, regulatory filing, or
on-record statement) or SPECULATIVE (one-outlet sourcing, however widely republished).

It changes how you read the landscape. This past week, the three biggest stories in AI
healthcare are all still SPECULATIVE. That itself tells you something — about where attention
is pointed before anyone's confirmed anything.

Platform is bigskylabs.ai — daily market digests for AI-in-healthcare, free to browse.

Does the CONFIRMED/SPECULATIVE distinction matter in how you use market news in your work,
or is the signal useful either way?
