## 1. Healthcare Investors Network

**Audience:** VC partners, associates, angels, healthcare-focused fund managers
**Angle:** The 95% undisclosed funding problem — investors work with fragmentary deal-flow
data, and the public picture of capital movement in AI healthcare is almost entirely
constructed from the visible 5%

---

110 AI-healthcare funding signals logged in my platform last week.

5 disclosed a dollar amount.

That's the public picture of capital flow in one of the most active sectors in tech right
now: 5% signal, 95% "a round was announced." The rounds that get numbers attached tend to
be the largest and most strategic — the ones where the company or lead investor specifically
wants the market to know the size. Everything else is noise with no measurement.

I track deal signals daily across 682 AI-healthcare companies — funding announcements, M&A,
partnerships, and regulatory events — and the non-disclosure rate is consistent week over
week. It's not a slow-news artifact. It's the baseline.

The implication: the market maps most funds have for AI healthcare are largely built from
the same visible 5%. The actual velocity of capital — how many deals are happening, at
what stage, in which sub-verticals — is only visible if you're watching the secondary
signals: hiring spikes, regulatory filings, new product clearances, partnership
announcements that imply a capital event without naming it.

That's what my platform tries to surface. It's at bigskylabs.ai — free to browse, daily
digest, deal signals section has the funding tracker.

Curious from people doing deals in this space: how much of your deal-flow picture actually
comes from public announcements vs. proprietary network sourcing? Is the 95%
non-disclosure rate a feature (less noise) or a problem (less data)?

"AI in healthcare is history being written right now. I'm building the archive."

## 2. Digital Health Investors

**Audience:** Investors specifically focused on digital health — health IT, healthtech,
telehealth, AI-in-care-delivery
**Angle:** The CONFIRMED vs. SPECULATIVE gap in deal reporting — why "reportedly acquired"
stays speculative longer than most investors realize, and what that costs in decision-making

---

Three of last week's top AI-healthcare headlines were still SPECULATIVE in my system when
they were already being cited as confirmed deals in investor updates and pitch decks.

That's not a criticism of anyone. It's just how information travels at speed: "reportedly"
disappears by the third republish. A rumored acquisition reads like a closed one within 48
hours, regardless of whether anyone's confirmed it.

I classify every headline in my daily AI-healthcare market digest as CONFIRMED (second
independent source, regulatory filing, or on-record statement) or SPECULATIVE (one-outlet
sourcing, however widely republished). The ratio surprises people: in a given week, 40–60%
of the major deal headlines in AI healthcare are still SPECULATIVE by my criteria even
after significant pickup.

The reason this matters for investors: if you're using market news to inform deal-pace
decisions — "the acquirer is active, we should move" — the CONFIRMED/SPECULATIVE
distinction is the difference between a real signal and a recycled rumor.

The digest is at bigskylabs.ai. CONFIRMED/SPECULATIVE labels are on every entry, with
source links.

For those active in digital health deals: do you have a process for distinguishing sourced
reporting from republished rumor, or does the distinction mostly not matter at your stage
of the deal?

"AI in healthcare is history being written right now. I'm building the archive."
