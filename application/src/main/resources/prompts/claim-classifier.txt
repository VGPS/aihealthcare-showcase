DO NOT extract:
- Claims made by third parties about AI companies
- General industry statistics
- Claims without a clearly identified company source
- Paraphrased editorials — only direct or strongly attributed company statements

For each claim you extract, assign:

CLAIM TYPE (choose one):
- CAPABILITY_CLAIM — model capabilities, accuracy, reasoning, or performance
- SAFETY_CLAIM — safety, alignment, harm reduction, or risk
- BENCHMARK_CLAIM — benchmark scores, leaderboard rankings, or evaluation results
- TIMELINE_CLAIM — release dates, roadmap milestones, or availability windows
- REGULATORY_CLAIM — regulatory approvals, compliance certifications, or clearances
- PARTNERSHIP_CLAIM — partnerships, integrations, or enterprise deployments

CLAIM VERDICT — use this decision logic in order:

1. MARKETING_HYPE — assign this when the claim uses promotional, comparative, or superlative language without citing supporting data. Examples of language that signals MARKETING_HYPE:
   - Explicit superlatives: "best", "most powerful", "most capable", "most advanced", "fastest", "first-of-its-kind", "number one"
   - Vague comparative: "industry-leading", "best-in-class", "state-of-the-art", "world-class", "next-generation", "cutting-edge" (without a citation)
   - Magnitude without data: "breakthrough", "revolutionary", "unprecedented", "groundbreaking", "transformative", "game-changing", "paradigm shift"
   - Vague capability claims: "can do almost anything", "understands like a human", "surpasses human-level", "solves complex problems" (without benchmark or study)
   - Implicit comparisons: "significantly better", "dramatically improved", "far superior" without citing what it was compared to or how
   Use MARKETING_HYPE even when the language is subtle. If the claim's primary purpose is promotional positioning rather than factual disclosure, it is MARKETING_HYPE.

2. EVIDENCE_BACKED — assign only when the article itself cites peer-reviewed research, an independent third-party benchmark study, or an independently verified audit that supports the claim. Self-reported benchmark scores without independent verification do NOT qualify.

3. CONTRADICTED — assign when the claim directly conflicts with other credible, sourced evidence referenced in the same or closely related articles (rare).

4. RETRACTED — assign when the company has explicitly corrected or withdrawn the claim (very rare).

5. ALLEGED_UNVERIFIED — assign as a last resort when the claim is a specific factual statement (a number, date, feature, or capability) stated by the company without independent citation, but using plain factual language rather than promotional language. If in doubt between ALLEGED_UNVERIFIED and MARKETING_HYPE, choose MARKETING_HYPE for language that is clearly promotional in intent.

DECISION RULE: Most claims will be either ALLEGED_UNVERIFIED (factual but uncited) or MARKETING_HYPE (promotional language). Before assigning ALLEGED_UNVERIFIED, ask: "Is this language promotional or comparative?" If yes, use MARKETING_HYPE.
