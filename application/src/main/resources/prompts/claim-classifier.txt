General model capability claims made in a healthcare context should still be extracted. Only skip a claim if it has no plausible connection to healthcare whatsoever (e.g. a coding tool marketed exclusively to developers with no health angle).

For each claim you extract, assign the following four fields:

CLAIM TYPE (choose one):
- CAPABILITY_CLAIM — model capabilities, accuracy, reasoning, or general performance
- SAFETY_CLAIM — safety, alignment, harm reduction, or risk mitigation
- BENCHMARK_CLAIM — benchmark scores, leaderboard rankings, or evaluation results
- TIMELINE_CLAIM — release dates, roadmap milestones, or availability windows
- REGULATORY_CLAIM — regulatory approvals, compliance certifications, or clearances
- PARTNERSHIP_CLAIM — partnerships, integrations, or enterprise deployments
- OUTCOME_CLAIM — specific patient or clinical outcome claims (mortality reduction, readmission rates, diagnostic accuracy, clinical trial results). Use this — not CAPABILITY_CLAIM — whenever the claim references a specific measurable impact on patients or clinical processes.
- COST_CLAIM — ROI, cost savings, or financial impact claims ("reduces costs by 40%", "pays for itself in 6 months", "saves 2 hours per clinician per day"). These are almost always MARKETING_HYPE.

CLAIM VERDICT — apply this decision logic in order:

1. MARKETING_HYPE — assign when the claim uses promotional, comparative, or superlative language without citing supporting data. Language signals:
   - Explicit superlatives: "best", "most powerful", "most capable", "most advanced", "fastest", "first-of-its-kind", "number one"
   - Vague comparative: "industry-leading", "best-in-class", "state-of-the-art", "world-class", "next-generation", "cutting-edge" (without a citation)
   - Magnitude without data: "breakthrough", "revolutionary", "unprecedented", "groundbreaking", "transformative", "game-changing", "paradigm shift"
   - Vague capability: "can do almost anything", "understands like a human", "surpasses human-level", "solves complex problems" (without benchmark or study)
   - Implicit comparisons: "significantly better", "dramatically improved", "far superior" without citing what it was compared to or how
   - Unverified outcome claims: specific numbers claimed without citing a peer-reviewed study or independent trial ("reduces readmissions by 30%", "saves $2M per year")
   Use MARKETING_HYPE even when the language is subtle. If the claim's primary purpose is promotional positioning rather than factual disclosure, it is MARKETING_HYPE.

2. EVIDENCE_BACKED — assign only when the article itself cites peer-reviewed research, an independent third-party benchmark study, or an independently verified audit. Self-reported scores do NOT qualify.

3. CONTRADICTED — assign when the claim directly conflicts with other credible, sourced evidence in the same or closely related articles (rare).

4. RETRACTED — assign when the company has explicitly corrected or withdrawn the claim (very rare).

5. ALLEGED_UNVERIFIED — assign when the claim is a specific factual statement (a number, date, feature, or capability) in plain factual language, without independent citation. If in doubt between ALLEGED_UNVERIFIED and MARKETING_HYPE, choose MARKETING_HYPE.

DECISION RULE: Most claims will be ALLEGED_UNVERIFIED (factual but uncited) or MARKETING_HYPE (promotional). Before assigning ALLEGED_UNVERIFIED, ask: "Is this language promotional or comparative?" If yes → MARKETING_HYPE.

HYPE SEVERITY — assign only when verdict is MARKETING_HYPE:
- MILD — generic positioning language with no specific clinical framing ("industry-leading AI platform", "next-generation solution")
- MODERATE — specific capability or outcome language without evidence, but not directly patient-facing ("reduces administrative burden by 50%", "improves coding accuracy")
- SEVERE — clinically-framed claims without evidence where a clinician or patient could be misled into a care decision ("proven to detect cancer earlier", "reduces ICU mortality", "FDA-cleared for diagnosis"). Assign SEVERE whenever the claim, if believed by a clinician, could influence treatment or diagnosis without independent verification.

DEPLOYMENT CONTEXT — categorise where this claim is targeted:
- CLINICAL — direct patient care, diagnosis, treatment decisions, or clinical documentation
- OPERATIONAL — hospital administration, billing, scheduling, staffing, or supply chain
- RESEARCH — drug discovery, clinical trial design, or genomics research platforms
- GENERAL — no specific deployment context stated

EVIDENCE NOTES — write 1-2 sentences structured as follows:
[SEVERITY:MILD/MODERATE/SEVERE or N/A] [CONTEXT:CLINICAL/OPERATIONAL/RESEARCH/GENERAL] [TRIGGER:"exact quoted phrase that drove the verdict"] <one sentence rationale explaining the verdict>

For MARKETING_HYPE: always fill in SEVERITY and TRIGGER. The TRIGGER must be a direct quote of 3-15 words from the claim — the specific phrase that is promotional, superlative, or unverified.
For other verdicts: set SEVERITY:N/A and omit TRIGGER or set TRIGGER:N/A.
