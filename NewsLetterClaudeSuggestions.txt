Here are some practical tips for building a strong AI & Healthcare newsletter:

Content Harvesting

Source diversity — Pull from a mix of academic journals (PubMed, NEJM, JAMA), industry news (STAT News, MedCity News, Fierce Healthcare), preprint servers (medRxiv), and regulatory sources (FDA.gov, CMS). Follow key researchers and institutions on LinkedIn and X/Twitter.

Use aggregators — Tools like Feedly, Exploding Topics, or even Google Alerts set to terms like "AI diagnostics," "clinical NLP," or "FDA AI clearance" can funnel stories to you automatically.

Build a reading rhythm — Set aside 30–60 min daily to scan and tag articles. Use a tool like Notion, Pocket, or Readwise to bookmark and annotate as you go.

Quality filters — Prioritize peer-reviewed or FDA-cleared findings over vendor press releases. For AI in healthcare especially, distinguish between "promising lab result" and "clinically validated."

Content Editing

Lead with the "so what" — Your readers (likely clinicians, health IT folks, or executives) are busy. Every article blurb should answer: Why does this matter to my practice or organization?

Translate the jargon — Bridge the gap between AI technical language and clinical/operational language. Avoid both pure ML jargon and oversimplified hype.

Fact-check aggressively — Healthcare misinformation causes real harm. Verify study sample sizes, funding sources, and whether findings have been replicated.

Maintain a consistent voice — Authoritative but approachable. Skeptical of hype but optimistic about genuine progress.

Formatting

Use a consistent structure — A proven layout: headline → 2–3 sentence summary → why it matters → link. Readers should be able to skim and dive deep at will.

Section by topic — Group content into recurring buckets like: Clinical AI News | Regulatory Updates | Research Roundup | Tools & Products | Opinion/Commentary.

Keep it scannable — Short paragraphs, bold key phrases, and clear section headers. Most readers are on mobile.

One featured story — Lead each issue with one deeper dive (300–500 words) on the most important story of the week. Everything else can be briefer.

Cadence & Workflow

Weekly > daily for a solo or small-team operation. Bi-weekly is fine to start — consistency beats frequency.

Batch your work — Harvest all week, write and edit on one day, schedule for the next morning.

Use a template — Lock in your layout in your email platform (Beehiiv, Substack, ConvertKit, etc.) so formatting is never a bottleneck.

Trust & Compliance Notes

Since you're in healthcare, a few extra considerations:

Never give medical advice — Make clear the newsletter is informational only.

Disclose conflicts — If covering a vendor or tool you have a relationship with, say so.

HIPAA awareness — If you ever include case-based content, ensure it's properly de-identified.
