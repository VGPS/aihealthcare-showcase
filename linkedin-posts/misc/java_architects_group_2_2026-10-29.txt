Every morning, my app runs a 14-step cascade after harvesting new articles: regulatory event matching, sentiment scoring, framework analysis, trend detection, deal signal detection, wiki compilation, embedding, market digest generation, and more — each one calling an LLM at least once.

Early on, one bad call (an LLM timeout during sentiment analysis) took the entire morning run down with it. Nothing after that step executed. Nobody got a newsletter that day.

The fix wasn't retries or circuit breakers — it was accepting that steps 6 through 14 have zero reason to know step 5 failed. So the orchestrator wraps every single step in its own try-catch, logs the failure, and moves to the next step regardless:

```java
for (PipelineStep step : steps) {
    try {
        step.run();
    } catch (Exception e) {
        log.error("Pipeline step {} failed, continuing", step.name(), e);
    }
}
```

It looks almost too simple to be "architecture." But the alternative — a single try-catch around the whole cascade, or worse, no isolation at all — means one flaky external API call decides whether your subscribers get content that day. Fourteen independent failure domains beat one shared one, every time, for a pipeline where steps don't actually depend on each other's success.

For the architects here: where's the line for you between "this needs a real resilience pattern (circuit breaker, bulkhead)" and "this just needs a try-catch at the right scope"?
