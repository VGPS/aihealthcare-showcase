Everyone assumes a system with 6 AI providers, 14+ independent pipelines, and dozens of domain concerns must be microservices under the hood. It's a single Maven module.

What actually gives you the "swap this piece without touching anything else" benefit people reach for microservices to get isn't network boundaries — it's interface boundaries. Every external dependency (each AI provider, the database, every HTTP source) sits behind a port interface in the domain layer; adapters implementing those ports live in infrastructure. Swapping an AI provider or adding a new data source touches one adapter and zero domain code — same practical benefit as a microservice boundary, without the deploy complexity, network latency, or distributed-tracing tax that comes with actually splitting it into separate services.

For a solo developer, that tradeoff isn't close. Microservices earn their complexity when you have multiple teams needing independent deploy cadences. One person doesn't have that problem — they have the opposite problem: more moving parts to operate solo.

For the full-stack folks here: where's your line for "this actually needs to be a separate service" versus "this just needs a clean interface boundary"?
