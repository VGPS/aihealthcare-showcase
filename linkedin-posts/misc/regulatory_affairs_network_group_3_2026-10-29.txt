State agencies don't always issue a press release when they amend a law. Sometimes the only evidence is that the official statute page's text changed.

Every official source URL in my legislation registry gets re-fetched weekly and SHA-256 hashed. If the hash changes, that's flagged as a content change requiring review — independent of whether any news outlet covered it. It's a crude mechanism (it can't tell you *what* changed, just *that* something did), but it catches the category of change that's easiest to miss: a quiet amendment to an existing statute, versus a brand-new bill that at least generates some press coverage on introduction.

For compliance-tracking purposes, "a law I thought I understood just changed and nobody told me" is a worse failure mode than "a new law exists that I haven't reviewed yet" — the second one you know you have a gap; the first one you don't know you have a gap until it costs you.

For the regulatory and compliance folks here: how much of your own source monitoring is press-driven versus direct source re-checks like this?
