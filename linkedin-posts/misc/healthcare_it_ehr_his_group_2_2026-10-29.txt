If an EHR note or an HIS record showed up with no author, no timestamp, and no source, it'd get rejected before it ever reached a chart. Most AI healthcare news and analysis gets published with exactly that level of traceability — a claim, no citation, no way to check if it's still true six months later.

I built the opposite on purpose: every claim in my platform's compiled knowledge base links back to the specific article, source, and date it came from. And when a new article contradicts something previously stated — a reversed FDA decision, a walked-back funding claim — the system flags it as a tracked contradiction instead of silently overwriting the old claim. Nothing gets deleted. The system of record keeps both versions, timestamped, so you can see exactly when and why something changed.

It's a small thing, but it's the same instinct that makes a good audit trail valuable in any record system: the value isn't just knowing what's true now, it's knowing what changed and when.

For the governance-minded people in this group: how do you evaluate the trustworthiness of an external data source before it influences a decision inside your systems?
