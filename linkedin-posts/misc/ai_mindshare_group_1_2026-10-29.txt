When I run the same query through 4 different LLMs (Claude, GPT, Perplexity, Gemini) and show all 4 syntheses side by side instead of picking a "winner," something useful happens: the places where the models disagree are usually more informative than the places where they agree.

Agreement across 4 independently-trained models on a factual claim is a decent confidence signal. Disagreement isn't noise to average away — it's usually pointing at genuine ambiguity in the underlying sources: conflicting reports, a claim that's still speculative, or a question that doesn't have a settled answer yet. I stopped trying to synthesize a single "best" answer across models and started treating the 4 outputs as 4 independent classifiers whose variance is itself the data point.

This is basically ensemble-method intuition (bagging, model variance as uncertainty estimate) applied to generative synthesis instead of classification — the ML version of "don't trust a single annotator."

For the data science folks here: do you have a go-to way of quantifying inter-model disagreement on open-ended generation (not classification), or is this still mostly a qualitative judgment call for everyone?
