No fancy model required for this one — just three time windows and a frequency count, and it's still one of the most useful signals in my platform.

Every week, I count keyword frequency across 30-day, 90-day, and 180-day windows for every article harvested. A keyword that's proportionally far more frequent in the last 30 days than its 180-day baseline gets flagged "rising." One that's dropped off gets flagged "fading." One that didn't exist in the 180-day window at all gets flagged "new." No sentiment model, no embeddings, no LLM call — just ratios across nested windows.

The reason it works better than it should: momentum (rate of change) is often a stronger signal than raw volume for "what's actually happening right now" versus "what's just been consistently discussed." A keyword at steady moderate volume for 6 months is old news even if its absolute count is high. A keyword that tripled in 30 days is the story, even at lower absolute volume.

For the data science / business analyst folks here: when you're building a trend/momentum signal, do you default to multi-window frequency ratios like this, or go straight to something more model-based (time-series decomposition, anomaly detection)?
