I put my solo-built app up for sale a few weeks ago. Here's what actually surprised me — not the things I expected to be hard.

I expected the hard part to be the pitch. It wasn't. The hard part was realizing how much of the business lived only in my head. Buyers don't want a demo — they want to know it runs without me. That meant writing down architecture decisions I'd never documented, explaining why certain features exist, and proving the thing keeps working when I'm not the one babysitting it.

The second surprise: NDAs and walkthrough requests came faster than I expected, and from more serious people than I expected. Nobody tells first-time sellers that the deal-mechanics learning curve (term sheets, diligence requests, what's normal vs. a red flag) is its own skill — separate from the skill of building the thing.

If you've built something solo and you're even casually thinking about an eventual exit: start documenting now, not when a buyer asks. The documentation is worth more as insurance than as a sales tool — most of what I wrote down I should have had anyway.

For founders here who've sold or tried to sell something they built alone — what's the one thing you wish someone told you before the first buyer conversation?
