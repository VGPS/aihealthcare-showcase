Most competitive decks for healthcare product teams are a feature-comparison table someone updates manually, every few months, right before a QBR. By the time it ships, two rows are already stale.

I replaced that with an LLM-scored framework: every tracked competitor gets scored across 6 fixed dimensions (pulled from their actual recent news coverage, not a sales page) — automatically, continuously, with a written rationale behind each score, not just a number. The scores move as the news does. A company that just had a rough regulatory quarter sees that reflected before the next board meeting references outdated positioning.

The part that actually changed how I use it: a static comparison table answers "who's ahead right now." A continuously-rescored one answers "who's gaining or losing ground" — which is the question product teams actually need answered for roadmap timing.

For the PMs here: how often does your competitive framework actually get refreshed in practice — truly continuous, or realistically just before planning cycles?
