I built an autonomous market intelligence platform. Solo. No team.

Here's what it does without me touching it:

→ Harvests AI-healthcare news daily from 40+ sources
→ Classifies every headline as CONFIRMED or SPECULATIVE
→ Tracks every FDA AI clearance and CMS rule change automatically
→ Monitors 58 state AI-health laws for amendments and effective dates
→ Scores sentiment across 698 companies in the directory
→ Sends a formatted daily market digest

None of that happens because I'm working. It runs on scheduled jobs and AI pipelines while I sleep.

What made this possible wasn't a revolutionary idea. It was the tools. AI coding assistants cut my development time by 60-70%. Spring AI handled the LLM integration. The architecture is straightforward: harvest → classify → store → surface.

The business model is just as simple: pick a vertical where intelligence is valuable but scattered, build the pipeline to aggregate and classify it, make the output readable. Healthcare AI is the vertical I picked. But the same pattern works anywhere — legal tech, climate, pharma, defense, real estate.

The platform is live at bigskylabs.ai. I built it to answer one question: what does AI in healthcare actually look like when you track all of it at once, not just the headlines?

The answer turns out to be a lot noisier, more speculative, and more interesting than the press releases suggest.

What vertical are you building the intelligence layer for?
