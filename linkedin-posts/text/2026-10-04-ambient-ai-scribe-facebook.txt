Ambient AI companies claim their tools save physicians 2 hours per day on documentation.

That sounds like a solved problem.

It isn't.

---

Independent studies average 25–35 minutes of actual time savings — not 2 hours.

One health system found documentation time dropped 31% for physicians — but nursing staff correction time for AI note errors went up 18%.

The time is being saved somewhere. The question is: where does it actually go?

---

In fee-for-service medicine: faster notes → more patients → more revenue. The ROI math closes.

In employed physician models: the health system captures the gain. The physician's documentation burden drops. Their RVU targets stay the same. Their compensation doesn't change.

Adoption resistance often isn't about the AI. It's about who benefits from the saved time.

---

In value-based care: volume doesn't drive revenue. The 2-hour savings only generates ROI if it reduces burnout, expands panel size, or improves quality scores — none of which appear in a 90-day vendor pilot.

The market is pricing every ambient AI deployment as if it's fee-for-service.

---

No one in the pilot is asking the right question: What does the saved time actually become?

More patients. Earlier departure. Less burnout. Or just different paperwork.

The vendors don't fund that study. Most health systems haven't built the measurement infrastructure to find out.
