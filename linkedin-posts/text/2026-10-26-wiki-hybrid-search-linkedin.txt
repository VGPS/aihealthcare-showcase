The AI Healthcare Knowledge Wiki just got smarter.

Until now, searching the wiki was exact-match keyword only. Type "FDA clearance" and you got pages with those exact words. Miss a phrase? Miss the page.

Starting today the wiki uses **hybrid search** — two legs running simultaneously:

→ **Keyword match** — pages whose title or tags contain your exact phrase still appear first, guaranteed.

→ **Semantic similarity** — remaining slots are filled by AI-powered vector search that understands *meaning*, not just words. Ask about "AI misdiagnosis liability" and you'll surface pages about FDA clearance frameworks, clinical trial evidence standards, and malpractice precedent — even if none of them use the word "liability."

---

Why it matters: the wiki is a synthesized knowledge base compiled from 3,100+ harvested articles across PubMed, FDA feeds, industry news, and competitor monitoring. It already tracks contradictions when new evidence reverses a prior claim — the first time I've seen that in a healthcare AI tool.

Hybrid search makes that depth actually discoverable. You don't have to know the exact vocabulary the AI used when it compiled a page. Describe the concept and let the vector similarity engine do the translation.
