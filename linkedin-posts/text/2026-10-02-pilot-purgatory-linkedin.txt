Every healthcare AI pilot succeeds.

The 50-bed pilot at the academic medical center. The 3-month proof of concept in radiology. The sepsis model trial in the ICU. Almost all of them work. The data looks good. The clinical champion is enthusiastic. The vendor gets a case study.

Then the contract goes to procurement for enterprise rollout.

And something happens that almost nobody in the vendor ecosystem talks about honestly:

→ The clinical champion who ran the pilot gets promoted, reassigned, or leaves. The institutional knowledge of how the tool actually works leaves with them.
→ The ROI calculation from the pilot used controlled conditions — dedicated implementation support, hand-picked patients, motivated staff. None of that transfers automatically to 200 physicians who weren't part of the pilot.
→ Operational ownership was never defined. Procurement got done; nobody owns "make sure 200 physicians are using this 12 months from now."
→ The vendor's implementation budget was front-loaded for the pilot as a sales tool. The enterprise rollout is now a customer success problem, not a sales problem — and customer success is usually staffed lighter.
→ The success metric from the pilot ("20% reduction in sepsis response time in one ICU") doesn't translate into a metric the CFO can track across the enterprise. So there's no organizational urgency to scale.

Healthcare AI has a pilot problem. It is not a technology problem. The technology that passed the pilot is the same technology that's now sitting underutilized after enterprise contract signature.

The single best predictor of whether a healthcare AI deployment actually scales I've seen across the market: whether the health system has a Chief AI Officer — or equivalent — with operational budget authority and accountability for post-deployment outcomes. Not a title. A budget line and a performance metric.

Health systems that have that person are getting ROI from healthcare AI at scale. Health systems that have a pilot committee and a procurement process are generating vendor case studies.
