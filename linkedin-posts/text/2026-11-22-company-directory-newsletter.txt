===== NEWSLETTER BODY =====

The AIHealthcare Company Directory is a live database of 103 AI healthcare companies
ranked by signal, not by sponsorship. It updates every night from the same nightly
pipeline that powers the app's research, deal alerts, and regulatory monitoring. No

This article describes how the directory works, what it tracks, and what a subscriber

---

HOW COMPANIES ENTER THE DIRECTORY

The discovery pipeline runs nightly. It harvests articles from over 110 RSS feeds
covering academic research, regulatory filings, clinical reporting, industry news, and
financial coverage. When a company name appears frequently enough across those sources,
the pipeline flags it as a candidate.

A separate AI step writes the company description — a synthesis of what the news record
says about what the company builds, who they serve, where they operate, and what claims
they have made publicly. The description updates when new information contradicts prior
content. No human curator decides which companies appear; the news record decides.

---

THE THREE SIGNALS THAT DRIVE THE RANKING

Every company earns a relevance score from three live signals:

🔥 Trending — article mention velocity over 90 days. Each mention adds 3 points, up to
a maximum of 30 points. A company with ten or more mentions in 90 days holds the top
Trending position in that sort.

💰 Funded — a confirmed funding event detected in the latest classification cycle adds
20 points. The Recently Funded sort shows companies that closed rounds in the current
detection window.

⚠ Watch — negative sentiment detected in recent articles subtracts 15 points. The
Watch List sort surfaces companies with accumulated negative news: regulatory flags,
acquisition failures, clinical incident reporting, public criticism.

The signals stack. A company that is trending, recently funded, and carrying clean
sentiment holds the top position on Relevance. A company that drew regulatory scrutiny
drops regardless of article volume.

THE SUBSCRIBER INTELLIGENCE PANEL

Sentiment and risk scoring — a numerical score derived from the LLM-classified article

Regulatory event monitoring — FDA 510(k) filings, De Novo decisions, CMS rulemakings,

OPTION B — "What 27 AI Medical Imaging Companies Show Us About the Field"
==================================================

===== NEWSLETTER BODY =====

The AIHealthcare Company Directory tracks 27 companies in the Medical Imaging &
Diagnostics sector. That number is worth pausing on.

Medical imaging is where AI in healthcare got its first real regulatory traction. The
FDA cleared its first AI-based medical imaging device in 2018 — IDx-DR, a retinal
imaging tool for diabetic retinopathy screening. In the years since, over 950 AI-enabled
medical devices have received FDA clearance, and imaging tools account for the majority.

27 companies being tracked in this one sector in 2026 is not a coincidence. It reflects
where the regulatory template is most established, where clinical validation pathways
are best understood, and — by extension — where it has been safest to raise capital and
deploy in clinical settings.

[Image: 2026-11-22-company-directory-sector-filter.png — Medical Imaging & Diagnostics
sector filter, 27 companies with signal badges and funding stage tags]

---

WHAT THE 27 COMPANIES BUILD

The Medical Imaging & Diagnostics cluster covers several distinct sub-specialties:

Radiology workflow automation — AI that processes CT scans, MRI studies, and X-rays to
flag abnormalities before a radiologist reads. These tools are typically positioned as
read prioritization or worklist optimization, not autonomous diagnosis. The FDA has
cleared several for specific imaging indications.

Oncology imaging — Platforms focused on cancer detection and staging from medical
images. Breast cancer, lung cancer, and colorectal cancer screening tools account for
the majority of companies in this sub-category.

Pathology AI — A smaller but highly funded cluster building tools for digital pathology:
AI that analyzes whole-slide images from biopsies for cancer presence, grading, and
biomarker detection.

Ophthalmology and retinal imaging — Tools for diabetic retinopathy screening, glaucoma
detection, and age-related macular degeneration risk stratification. This sub-specialty
has the most regulatory clarity — retinal AI tools cleared by the FDA are in routine
clinical use at scale.

Dermatology screening — Consumer-facing and clinical tools for skin lesion analysis.
This cluster has the most regulatory complexity due to questions about scope of practice
when tools are deployed without direct physician oversight.

---

HOW THE DIRECTORY TRACKS THEM

Each of the 27 companies earns a live relevance score from three signals that update
nightly:

🔥 Trending — article velocity over 90 days (+3 pts per mention, max 30)
💰 Funded — a confirmed funding event detected in the latest scan (+20 pts)
⚠ Watch — negative sentiment in recent articles (-15 pts)

[Image: 2026-11-22-company-directory-overview.png — Full directory grid showing signal
badges across all 103 companies; Medical Imaging companies visible in upper rows]

The Watch signal matters in imaging AI specifically. Several companies in this sector
have been subject to published systematic reviews questioning performance on
underrepresented patient populations. That criticism lives in the academic article
record. The pipeline finds it.

---

WHY IMAGING AI IS A LEADING INDICATOR

Watching the 27 companies in this sector over time is not just watching the imaging AI
market. It is watching the FDA regulatory template develop.

How the FDA has regulated imaging AI — what it requires for clinical validation studies,
what labeling it demands, how it handles software modifications post-clearance — sets
the precedent for how it will regulate AI clinical decision support, AI documentation
tools, and AI drug discovery platforms.

The imaging AI market is ahead of the rest of AI healthcare by approximately six to
eight years of regulatory experience. The lessons from that experience are what every
other AI healthcare category will eventually work through.

OPTION C — "Why I Let the News Record Do the Curation"
==================================================

===== NEWSLETTER BODY =====

Most vendor directories have a curation problem. Someone decides who gets listed,
in what order, and with what description. That someone is either a paid analyst or
the vendor itself.

The AIHealthcare Company Directory does not work that way.

No company has applied to be in the directory. No vendor has paid for placement. No
human curator has ranked the results. 103 AI healthcare companies appear in this
directory because the news record — the daily flow of academic papers, regulatory
filings, deal announcements, and clinical reporting — put them there.

[Image: 2026-11-22-company-directory-overview.png — Full 103-company directory grid,
default Relevance sort, signal badges visible]

---

HOW THE CURATION WORKS WITHOUT A CURATOR

The discovery pipeline runs every night. It harvests articles from over 110 RSS feeds
covering the full range of AI healthcare coverage: academic research, FDA regulatory
filings, industry news, financial reporting, and clinical literature.

When a company name appears often enough across those sources, the pipeline identifies
it as a candidate and adds a record to the directory. When a company closes a funding
round, that event is detected by the deal classification pipeline and reflected in the
score within 24 hours. When a company draws criticism in a published systematic review,
the sentiment pipeline finds it and the score reflects that too.

The company description is written by an AI summarization step that synthesizes what
the public record says: what the company builds, who it serves, where it operates, and
what it has claimed publicly. The description updates when new information contradicts
prior content.

Three signals drive the ranking:

🔥 Trending — article velocity over 90 days (up to +30 pts)
💰 Funded — confirmed deal detected in latest cycle (+20 pts)
⚠ Watch — negative sentiment from recent articles (-15 pts)

---

WHAT THIS APPROACH GETS RIGHT

The pipeline does not distinguish between a company with a good PR budget and one
operating quietly but appearing frequently in clinical and regulatory literature. It
treats every mention in the news record equally, weighted only by recency and sentiment.

That means a company doing rigorous published clinical validation — without press
releases — appears in the directory ranked appropriately. A company with heavy
marketing presence but limited clinical evidence does not get a sponsored lift.

It also means bad news is tracked. When a company draws a negative CMS coverage
determination, a critical academic review, or a regulatory flag — the Watch signal
fires and the score drops. That does not happen in a directory where vendors control
their own listings.

THE GOAL IS A DIRECTORY THAT DOES NOT GO STALE

Manually curated directories go stale. Vendors change, merge, pivot, or fold —
and the curator does not always notice.

A directory driven by the news record updates itself. When a company stops appearing
in the article record, its Trending score decays. When it closes or gets acquired,
the event appears in the deal signal pipeline. When it pivots into a new category,
the pipeline recategorizes it.

The goal is a directory that reflects the current state of AI healthcare, not the
state it was in when someone last got around to updating a spreadsheet.
