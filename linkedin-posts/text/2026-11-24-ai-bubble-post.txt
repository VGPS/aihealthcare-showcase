OPTION B — "91% Accuracy. 70,000 Records. Bladder Cancer Found 5 Years Early."
==================================================

===== POST BODY =====

An AI model analyzed the medical records of nearly 70,000 patients and detected
bladder cancer up to five years before diagnosis.

The model — PRECISE-AGZ, University of Plymouth — analyzed electronic health records
collected between 1995 and 2020. It sifted through 48,261 potential health indicators
to identify 38 features that signal bladder cancer risk. Final accuracy: 91%.

This is not a new imaging device. This is not a novel biomarker that requires a
separate test order. PRECISE-AGZ runs against data already sitting in existing EHR
systems — medication histories, comorbidities, clinical activities, lifestyle
indicators.

The model also uncovered a nuance with real referral implications: blood in urine,
the standard primary trigger for bladder cancer investigation, was associated with
lower cancer risk when it appeared alongside benign prostate enlargement in men.
That finding could reduce unnecessary referrals for a large patient population.

Bladder cancer kills 220,000 people globally each year. Many cases are caught late
because early symptoms overlap with more common conditions. Stage 1 survival rates
exceed 90%. Stage 4 drops below 15%.

This was published in IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering. This is not a demo.
