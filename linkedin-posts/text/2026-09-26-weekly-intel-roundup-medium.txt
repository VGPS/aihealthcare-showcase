On September 22nd, PeaceHealth — a nonprofit health system operating hospitals across Washington, Oregon, and Alaska — filed a notice that it was cutting 250 IT jobs and moving the work to Tech Mahindra and HCI Group. It's a single line in a WARN Act database. Nobody covered it as a healthcare AI story.

But look at what happened in the same seven-day window: Amazon formally unveiled Connect Health, its agentic AI platform for point-of-care workflows. Anthropic published another update on Claude's positioning in healthcare and life sciences. Google pushed a new release of its Check Up Health AI initiative. And four separate states — Washington, California, Texas, New York — logged fresh WARN Act filings tied to healthcare IT layoffs and offshoring.

That's not a coincidence of timing. It's the same story told from two different data sources.

---

Two signals, one story

Every major cloud and AI vendor is racing to own the clinical workflow. That part of the story gets covered — extensively. What doesn't get covered is the other half: the in-house healthcare IT labor being displaced as health systems adopt AI-enabled outsourcing and offshore delivery models.

State WARN Act filings are public record. Every state with an at-scale layoff reporting requirement publishes them — usually as a dry, unindexed government database nobody outside of labor-law compliance teams reads regularly. They're not glamorous. They don't show up in a Google News search for "healthcare AI." But they are one of the only real-time, ground-truth signals of where healthcare IT labor is actually moving, and they consistently lead trade-press coverage of the same layoffs by days or weeks.

---

Why this matters if you cover, invest in, or work in healthcare AI

If you're an analyst or investor, the vendor announcements tell you where the market is going. The WARN filings tell you what it's already costing, and who's paying it. Both halves matter, and most healthcare AI coverage only tracks one of them.

If you're inside a health system's IT organization, this is the leading edge of a trend, not an isolated event. PeaceHealth isn't the first health system to outsource IT to AI-enabled delivery partners, and the state filing data suggests it won't be the last.
