--- SUBJECT LINE ---

My Database Rates AI Healthcare Companies by How the News Covers Them. Some Rankings Are Uncomfortable.

--- POST BODY ---

I built a system that reads every article in my database and assigns each AI healthcare company a sentiment score. Not a score based on press releases. Not based on what they told an analyst on an earnings call. Based on what journalists, researchers, and regulatory filings say about them — classified at the article level, aggregated to a company score.

Each article gets a label: POSITIVE, NEGATIVE, MIXED, or NEUTRAL. The classification comes with a confidence score and a rationale. The company score rolls all of them up: percentage positive, percentage negative, percentage mixed, and a risk summary that flags patterns — the same concern appearing across multiple independent sources, gaps between what a company claims and what third parties report, regulatory mentions that skew negative consistently.

Some of the results are not what I expected.

Companies that have been loudest about their healthcare AI momentum have some of the highest negative-sentiment concentrations in my database. The coverage isn't hostile — most of it is accurate reporting of friction points appearing repeatedly: clinical workflow integration challenges, gaps between benchmark performance and real-world deployment, regulatory questions that keep surfacing without resolution.

The recurrence is the signal. One negative article is noise. The same concern appearing across eight independent sources over four months is a pattern. The system catches the pattern whether or not the company's communications team does.

The other thing the system does — which turns out to matter — is separate sentiment at the article level before aggregating. A company with 90 POSITIVE articles and 10 NEGATIVE articles has a different risk profile than a company with 50 MIXED articles, even if the raw positive percentage is similar. The distribution tells a different story than the mean. A single persistent negative thread in an otherwise positive corpus is a flag. The aggregate score alone misses it.

This is the kind of signal that doesn't show up in a vendor demo. It doesn't show up in a category report written by a firm that consults for the vendors it covers. It shows up when you read what gets published about a company across enough sources, consistently enough, over long enough.

The full sentiment dashboard is at app.bigskylabs.ai — company-by-company scores, the article-level breakdown behind each one, and the risk summary for any company where negative patterns cross a threshold. The scores update every time new coverage lands.
