When a competitor closes a deal, most people read the headline and move on.

"$40M Series B. AI-powered clinical documentation. Existing investors participated."

That tells you almost nothing about whether the company is compounding or cornered.
The context is what changes the reading — and it's usually filed in four different places.

So I built a cross-reference that assembles it automatically.

Open any deal signal and you see:
• Their sentiment picture — how coverage has been trending across POSITIVE / NEGATIVE / MIXED,
  with per-article rationale so you can read the reasoning, not just the score
• Their competitive framework — scored across six dimensions (clinical validation, regulatory
  position, market adoption, technical depth, data assets, partnerships) and rendered as a
  radar chart so the shape reads in five seconds
• Regulatory exposure — any active FDA or CMS activity that touches them
• Company profile — discovery date, article velocity, sector, recent coverage

The interesting cases are the mismatches.

A large raise into declining sentiment. A quiet round at a company with unusually strong
framework scores. A deal that looks routine until you notice they have an open FDA action
in the same category.

None of those are visible from the deal feed alone.

One event. Four lenses. The mismatches are where the analysis lives.
