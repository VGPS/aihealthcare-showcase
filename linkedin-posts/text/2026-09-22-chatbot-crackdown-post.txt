=== SUBSTACK POST ===

Title: The AI Therapy Crackdown: 16 States Drew a Hard Line on Chatbot Companions
Subtitle: What the emerging chatbot regulation wave means for digital health builders, health plans, and anyone deploying conversational AI

---

Millions of people have been using AI companion chatbots as therapists.

Not because they were told to. Not because the apps were marketed as clinical tools. But because the experience was good enough — available at 2 a.m., judgment-free, cheaper than a copay, and always listening. The line between "emotional support app" and "de facto therapy" blurred quietly, and then completely.

Sixteen state legislatures noticed. Between 2025 and 2026, they passed 16 laws specifically targeting AI companion chatbots — with requirements that are now in force or taking effect soon.

Here is what the data shows.

**The Three Universal Requirements**

Every one of the 16 laws converges on the same three mandates:

*Disclosure of AI identity.* Chatbots must clearly inform users they are interacting with software, not a human — at the start of every session, and repeatedly for minor users. California (AB 489) makes it explicitly illegal to use any title, term, or letter implying that a licensed health care professional is on the other end. Violations are a professional conduct matter.

*Crisis detection and referral.* Operators must maintain real-time protocols capable of detecting expressions of suicidal ideation, self-harm, or threatened harm to others — and responding with immediate crisis referrals. This is not advisory guidance. In California, Colorado, Connecticut, Georgia, Hawaii, Idaho, Iowa, Nebraska, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Utah, and Washington, it is now a legal obligation.

*Heightened minor protections.* Most laws require more frequent AI disclosures for users under 18 — hourly in many states, every session in others. Several require age verification. Oregon goes further: it prohibits engagement-maximizing design patterns for minor users altogether.

**The Two Outliers**

Two states passed laws stricter than the rest.

Nevada (AB 406, effective July 2025) does not regulate how AI chatbots must behave — it prohibits AI from providing services that would constitute the practice of professional mental or behavioral health care at all. The distinction matters: Nevada drew a categorical line. AI can be supportive; it cannot be a provider.

California (AB 489, effective January 2026) targets impersonation directly. It is unlawful for AI to use professional terms, titles, or letters — anything that implies a licensed clinician is involved. A chatbot named "Dr. Ava" or one that signs off with "Licensed Therapist" is now in violation.

**Why This Wave Happened Now**

The regulatory response tracks the adoption curve. AI companion apps — Character.AI, Replika, and dozens of healthcare-adjacent products — saw explosive user growth between 2023 and 2025. Several high-profile incidents involving minors using AI companions during mental health crises brought the issue to legislative attention simultaneously across multiple states.

The result was a remarkably consistent set of bills, most modeled on shared template language developed by Manatt Health and similar policy groups. When 14 states pass nearly identical laws in the same legislative cycle, it reflects coordinated policy diffusion, not coincidence.

**What's Still Coming**

Eight of the 16 laws take effect January 1, 2027 or later. Colorado (HB 26-1263), Georgia (SB 540), Idaho (S 1297), Nebraska (LB 525), New York (Safe by Design Act), Oregon (SB 1546), Rhode Island (S 2195), and Washington (HB 2225) are all still in the implementation window.

More states are likely to follow. The Manatt Health AI policy tracker — one of the sources we harvest weekly — has flagged at least four additional states with companion chatbot bills in committee for the 2027 legislative session.

**What It Means If You Build or Deploy Conversational AI in Healthcare**

The compliance question is no longer theoretical. If your product:

- provides emotional support, mental health content, or behavioral health guidance
- serves any users who may be minors
- uses conversational or companion-style UX patterns

...then at least some of these 16 laws apply to you, depending on which states your users are in.

The minimum viable compliance posture today: AI disclosure at session start, crisis detection with documented referral protocols, and minor-user safeguards. The full compliance picture varies by state and will require legal review of each jurisdiction's specific language.
