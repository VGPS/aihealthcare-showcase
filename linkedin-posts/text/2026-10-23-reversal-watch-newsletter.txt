--- POST BODY ---

# The Record That Corrects Itself

Most news trackers have one job: capture the claim. Mine has a second job almost nobody else builds for: notice when a later claim contradicts an earlier one, and keep both.

That's not a bug-tracking feature. It's the actual argument for building a historical archive of AI in healthcare instead of just another headline feed.

## Why This Needed to Exist

Healthcare AI coverage moves fast, and "fast" and "self-correcting" are not the same thing. A company's "FDA clearance pending" from June quietly becomes "no FDA submission on file" by September, and nothing in the news cycle flags the reversal — the June article just scrolls out of view. A vendor's "zero reported adverse events" claim from a product launch gets contradicted by a safety bulletin four months later, read by a completely different audience.

If you weren't watching both moments, you never see the correction. You just see two unrelated facts, months apart, with no thread connecting them.

## How Reversal Watch Works

Every article that enters the pipeline gets compiled into the knowledge wiki — a persistent, LLM-maintained set of pages per company, concept, and comparison. Each page tracks its own claims with full source provenance: which article, which outlet, which date, with an excerpt you can check against the original.

When a new source contradicts a claim already on a page, the system doesn't quietly overwrite it. It records a contradiction: the prior claim, the new claim, and the sources backing each side. Both claims stay visible. Nothing gets memory-holed.

The result is a live "Reversal Watch" feed — a running list of exactly where the AI healthcare narrative flipped, and on what evidence.

## See It Yourself

This isn't a claim I'm asking you to take on faith. The wiki and the contradiction feed are public, no account required:

→ app.bigskylabs.ai/wiki/contradictions — every tracked reversal, with both sides sourced
→ app.bigskylabs.ai/wiki — the underlying knowledge pages, each one carrying its own provenance trail

Go look. If a page has a contradiction on it, it's shown — not buried in a changelog, not something you have to request.

The wiki compiles from the same pipeline that tracks 692 companies in the directory and 58 state AI-health laws across 27 states. Reversal Watch isn't a side feature bolted onto that — it's what happens to the same evidence once you keep enough of it to compare today's claim against yesterday's.

## Why I Think This Matters More Than Another Dashboard

Dashboards tell you what's true right now. An archive that tracks reversals tells you how confident you should be in what's true right now — because it shows you how often "true right now" has already changed once.

That's the actual value of a longitudinal record over a news feed: not more data, but a memory long enough to catch itself being wrong, and honest enough to leave the record showing it.
