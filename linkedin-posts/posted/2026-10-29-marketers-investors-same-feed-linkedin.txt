Two people opened my app this morning. Neither one cares about the other's job.

One is a marketer at a healthcare AI vendor. She doesn't need analysis — she needs to know what's moving right now so this week's content calendar isn't talking about last month's news. She pulls up the trend page: which keywords are rising over the last 30 days versus the last 180, which topics just went from "new" to "accelerating." That's her brief, built automatically, every Sunday.

The other is an investor doing diligence on a Series C healthcare AI company before a term sheet. He doesn't want a press release — he wants a real-time read: has this company shown up in a deal signal (funding round, M&A rumor, partnership) in the last 90 days? What does the sentiment trend on their coverage actually look like, article by article, not just "mostly positive"? How does their 6-dimension competitive score compare to the three companies they're being pitched against?

Same underlying feed. Same harvested articles, same LLM pipeline. What changes is the lens.

The marketer's lens is velocity — what's rising, what's fading, what's brand new this week. 
The investor's lens is signal; funding events, sentiment risk flags, competitive positioning scored against named peers, all attached to a specific company slug and refreshed continuously instead of once a quarter when the 10-Q drops.

Most tools pick one audience and optimize for it. This one didn't have to choose, because the hard part — turning a flood of healthcare AI news into structured, attributed, queryable data — is identical either way. The output format is just a different cut of the same table.
