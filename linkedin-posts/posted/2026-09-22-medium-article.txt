I Built an AI Healthcare Intelligence Platform in 6 Months. Here's What I Learned.

My wife died from cancer earlier this year.

During those months in hospitals and clinics, I watched doctors and staff struggle to
keep pace with a wave of AI tools entering healthcare — new treatments, new diagnostic
software, shifting regulations — while the information about all of it was scattered
across dozens of sources. Nobody had a clear picture. Not the clinicians. Not the
administrators. Not the patients.

I'm a 72-year-old full-stack Java developer. The job market has made clear it doesn't
want me anymore. But grief has a way of clarifying priorities, and I decided to apply
everything I've learned over 30 years to build something that mattered.

Six months later, BigSkyLabs.ai is live.

[IMAGE 1 — directory-hero.png]

--- What It Does ---

BigSkyLabs.ai is an AI-powered healthcare intelligence platform. It automatically
harvests articles from academic, regulatory, and industry sources every day, processes
them with large language models, and surfaces structured intelligence across four
major features.

--- The Company Directory ---

The platform tracks 459 AI healthcare companies, ranked daily by a live relevance
score calculated from three signals: article velocity (how often a company appears
in recent news), funding activity (M&A and investment deal signals), and sentiment
(positive vs. negative press tone).

Every morning, the pipeline re-scores all companies automatically. Analysts can sort
by trending activity, recent funding, or sentiment risk — and export the full dataset
to CSV.

[IMAGE 2 — directory-cards.png]

--- The Knowledge Wiki ---

This was the part I underestimated. After six months of daily article harvesting,
the platform has auto-compiled an 840-page wiki covering AI healthcare companies,
concepts, regulations, and clinical applications.

Each page carries source provenance — you can trace every claim back to the original
article. The wiki also tracks contradictions: when a new article reverses a prior
claim, the system flags it automatically.

This is not static documentation. It updates itself.

[IMAGE 3 — wiki-index.png]

--- The Legislation Tracker ---

Health AI regulation is accelerating. As of today, 41 laws across 24 U.S. states
have been enacted governing AI in healthcare — covering everything from prior
authorization algorithms to AI disclosure requirements for clinical tools to
mental health chatbot restrictions.

The platform seeds from a 43-record researched dataset and runs a weekly Perplexity
Search discovery sweep for newly enacted or amended bills. A source-freshness monitor
re-fetches official bill URLs weekly and flags content changes for human review.

[IMAGE 4 — legislation-map.png]

--- The Technical Stack ---

I built this on what I know best, extended with what the moment required:

- Spring Boot 3 / Java 21 — hexagonal architecture, ports and adapters
- Spring AI — ChatClient abstraction over Anthropic Claude and OpenAI
- PostgreSQL 16 + pgvector — article storage, vector embeddings, semantic search
- Perplexity Sonar API — live web research for market news and legislation discovery
- Alpaca Markets API — real-time market data for publicly traded healthcare AI companies
- Thymeleaf — server-rendered UI (no React, no microservices, one JVM)
- AWS EC2 + SES — self-hosted, cost-efficient for the current scale

The entire pipeline — harvest, summarize, classify, embed, score, notify — runs on
a $50/month EC2 instance. The LLM costs are the dominant variable expense.

--- What I Got Wrong ---

I underestimated how much the data quality problem would dominate the engineering
problem. Getting LLMs to produce reliable structured output at scale — consistent
JSON schemas, no hallucinated company names, deterministic sentiment labels — took
more iteration than any single feature.

I also underestimated the copyright surface area. An article ingestion pipeline
that stores full body text creates legal exposure. I built an excerpt truncation
layer mid-project — capping RSS bodies at 2,000 characters and web-scraped content
at 500, with robots.txt compliance for every domain. Retrofitting safety into an
existing pipeline is harder than building it in from the start.

--- What Surprised Me ---

The legislation tracker became the most-visited public page almost immediately.
Healthcare compliance teams need this data and there's no good free source for it.

The wiki's contradiction detection — flagging when new articles reverse prior claims
— turned out to be more valuable than I anticipated. The FDA changes guidance.
CMS reverses reimbursement decisions. Clinical trial results contradict earlier
findings. Having a system that notices these reversals automatically is genuinely useful.

--- What's Next ---

The platform is live at app.bigskylabs.ai with a free tier and a $49/month
subscriber plan. I'm focused on growing the user base and reaching healthcare
market research teams who need this kind of structured intelligence.

If you work in healthcare AI — as a researcher, analyst, investor, or product
leader — I'd welcome your feedback.

And if you're a senior developer who's been told you're past your expiration date:
the technology doesn't agree. Build something.
