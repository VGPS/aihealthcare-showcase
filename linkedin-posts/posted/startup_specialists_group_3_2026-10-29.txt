I haven't manually written a LinkedIn post for my product's marketing in a few weeks. The product writes them.

Every day, my app's pipeline pulls fresh market data — funding rounds, FDA actions, company signals — and an AI agent drafts LinkedIn and Facebook posts straight from that day's findings. I review, tweak if needed, post. That's it. The marketing engine and the product are the same pipeline; I didn't build a separate "content team" layer, I just pointed the existing data pipeline at a new output.

The unlock wasn't the AI writing — it was realizing the content doesn't need a separate source of truth. If your product already generates interesting data daily, the marketing is a formatting problem, not a creativity problem. Most solo founders I talk to are still treating "building the product" and "marketing the product" as two jobs that compete for the same hours. They don't have to be two jobs if the product's own output is the content.

This obviously doesn't work for every product — you need something that generates a genuinely new, interesting fact on a regular cadence. But if yours does, you're probably sitting on a content engine you haven't pointed at anything yet.

What does your product generate daily that you haven't thought to turn into content?
