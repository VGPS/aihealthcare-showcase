The fastest way to lose money on M&A chatter is treating a "sources say talks are underway" headline the same as a signed definitive agreement. They're not the same information, but they read almost identically in a news feed at 2am.

Every single market-moving item in my platform gets explicitly tagged CONFIRMED or SPECULATIVE before it ever reaches the digest — "confirmed" means a completed transaction, an actual regulatory filing, or an official earnings release; "speculative" means rumor, unconfirmed report, or analyst conjecture, no matter how credible the source. The two categories never get blended into one undifferentiated news stream.

It sounds almost too basic to be a real architecture decision. But I'd bet most people's mental model of "what happened in AI healthcare this week" is quietly contaminated with a few speculative items they've started treating as settled fact, simply because nothing forced the distinction at the point of consumption.

See the live digest with every item tagged either way. 👉 Link in the first comment.

For the investors and operators here: do you have an explicit confirmed-vs-speculative filter in your own information diet, or is that distinction mostly implicit (source reputation, gut feel)?
