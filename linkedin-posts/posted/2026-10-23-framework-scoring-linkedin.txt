Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and AWS are all pitching themselves as healthcare AI platforms right now. Every one of them picks its own highlight reel to prove it.

So I stopped reading highlight reels and built a scorecard that doesn't know which company it's grading.

Six dimensions, same rubric, every time: technical maturity, clinical validation, regulatory positioning, platform strategy, market momentum, and developer experience. Each one scored 1–10 against the actual source articles — not vendor marketing, not my opinion. If a dimension has no evidence behind it for a given company, the score says so instead of guessing.

That's the part that matters. A vendor comparison you read in a press release always flatters the vendor who wrote it. A rubric applied the same way to four competitors, pulling from the same evidence pool, can't play favorites — it just scores what's actually documented.

The output is a radar chart per company and a paragraph assessment that names gaps as directly as strengths. If a platform's regulatory story is thin, the scorecard says thin. If a platform's clinical validation is strong, it says strong. Same standard, both directions.

This is the kind of comparison a health system's AI vendor committee pays a consulting firm to produce once a quarter. Mine updates every time new coverage lands.
