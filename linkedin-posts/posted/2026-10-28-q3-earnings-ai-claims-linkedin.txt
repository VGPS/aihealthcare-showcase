Q3 earnings season is wrapping up. Every major healthcare AI company spent time on their call talking about AI — AI-driven revenue growth, AI adoption by health systems, AI as a category-defining competitive moat.

The price reaction log disagrees with several of them.

I track market-moving AI healthcare news events across four time horizons: 1 hour, 4 hours, 1 day, 3 days. Every earnings report that includes a material AI claim gets logged. So does the price delta at each window.

Here is what Q3 produced:

Companies that reported specific, auditable AI metrics — number of health systems live on AI documentation, measured reduction in physician time on notes, named contracts with reference customers — tended to see moves that held at 72 hours. Not large moves, but durable ones. The market processed the claim and believed it.

Companies that reported directional AI language — "accelerating AI adoption," "strong AI pipeline," "positioned to lead as AI transforms care delivery" — without attaching numbers to any of it, tended to see flat or reversed moves. The 4-hour window was often the tell. Initial sentiment formed and then corrected itself.

The non-reaction is the signal most people skip past. A company whose CEO spent 8 minutes of prepared remarks on AI strategy and saw a 0.1% price move is telling you something. The market has an opinion about the claim. It differs from the press release.

This is not a bearish take on healthcare AI. It is a precision take. The sector is real. The companies with actual deployed systems, actual contract revenue, actual measured outcomes are separating from the ones still in the "AI-first future" portion of their narrative.
