A pattern that shows up consistently across the healthcare AI market and almost never gets said plainly:

Every healthcare AI pilot succeeds. The 50-bed ICU sepsis trial. The radiology AI proof of concept. The ambient documentation pilot in one clinic. Almost all of them produce positive results. The vendor gets a case study. The clinical champion declares victory.

Then the enterprise contract gets signed and the rollout stalls.

Not because the technology failed. The same product that worked in the pilot is sitting underutilized 12 months after enterprise signature. Something else happened.

Here's what actually kills healthcare AI at scale, based on what shows up consistently in the market:

The clinical champion who ran the pilot leaves or gets promoted. They were the institutional glue — they knew why the tool worked, who to call when it didn't, and how to get physicians to actually use it. When they're gone, none of that transferred.

The ROI metric from the pilot doesn't scale. "20% reduction in sepsis response time in one ICU" is a great case study line. It is not a CFO-trackable metric across a 12-hospital system, and without that metric, there's no organizational urgency to keep pushing adoption.

Operational ownership was never defined. Procurement got done. Nobody owns "make sure 200 physicians are using this in 18 months" as a job responsibility with budget and accountability.

Vendor implementation support was front-loaded for the pilot because the pilot was a sales tool. Enterprise rollout is a customer success problem — and customer success is staffed lighter than sales.

This is not a technology critique. Healthcare AI has gotten genuinely better, faster. This is an organizational design critique: health systems are built to run pilots, not to scale them. The procurement process, the committee structure, the champion model — all of it is optimized for evaluation, not deployment.

The one structural variable I've seen correlate with successful enterprise AI deployment: a Chief AI Officer or equivalent who has operational budget authority and a performance metric tied to post-deployment outcomes. Not just a title. An actual budget line and accountability.

Health systems with that person are getting ROI from AI at scale. Health systems with a pilot committee are generating vendor case studies.
