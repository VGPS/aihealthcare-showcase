--- TITLE ---

Three Big AI-Healthcare Headlines Hit Today. My System Flagged All Three as Unconfirmed.

--- POST BODY ---

Three stories moved through the AI-healthcare news cycle today:

- EliseAI reportedly raising $350M at a $4B valuation
- Oracle and AstraZeneca reportedly exploring AI and real-world data for clinical trials
- OpenAI reportedly acquiring a company called Orchestra Bio

Every one of them is getting covered like settled fact. In my system, every one of them is tagged SPECULATIVE, not CONFIRMED.

That's not a knock on the reporting — it's a distinction that gets lost the moment a story starts getting reshared. CONFIRMED means the deal shows up in a second independent source, a regulatory filing, or an on-record company statement. SPECULATIVE means it's still running on one outlet's sourcing, no matter how many places have since repeated it.

I built that classification into the pipeline on purpose. AI-healthcare news moves fast enough, and the incentive to publish first is strong enough, that "reportedly" quietly disappears by the third republish. A $350M raise reads like a done deal. An "exploring AI" partnership reads like a signed one. None of that is malicious — it's just what happens to information at speed, and it's exactly the kind of drift a historical record needs to resist.

I track this across a growing base: 682 companies in the directory, 58 state AI-health laws, and a daily digest that cross-checks every headline against a second source before it gets labeled either way. Today, the three biggest stories all landed on the SPECULATIVE side of that line. That tells you something too — where the industry's attention is pointed before anyone's actually confirmed anything.

If EliseAI's raise closes, if Oracle and AstraZeneca sign something, if OpenAI's acquisition goes through — the record will show it moved from SPECULATIVE to CONFIRMED, with the date it happened. That's the whole point of keeping one.

"AI in healthcare is history being written right now. I'm building the archive."
