--- SUBJECT LINE ---

Marketers Need What's Timely. Investors Need What's True Right Now. Same Database Answers Both.

--- POST BODY ---

I built this as a newsletter engine. It's turned into two different tools wearing the same coat, depending on who's logged in.

The common foundation is the same for everyone: a pipeline that harvests AI-healthcare news daily from PubMed, FDA/CMS feeds, industry press, and competitor pages, classifies and scores every article, and stores it with full source attribution. What happens after that split cleanly into two use cases I didn't originally design for.

**The marketer's version of the problem is: what's timely?**

A content calendar built a month in advance is a content calendar talking about last month. The trend detection pipeline runs weekly, scoring keyword frequency across three rolling windows — 30, 90, and 180 days — and classifying each keyword as rising, fading, or brand new. "Ambient scribe fatigue" might be flat for 90 days and then spike in the last 30 — that's a rising signal worth a post this week, not a retrospective next quarter. The historical trend archive keeps every snapshot, so you can also see which "rising" keywords six months ago turned out to be real versus noise — useful for not chasing every spike.

The market digest adds a second timeliness layer specifically for anything that moved a stock price or triggered a regulatory action: ranked, fact-versus-speculation-separated entries with a price-reaction log across four horizons (1 hour, 4 hours, 1 day, 3 days). A marketer writing about "what just happened" has an auditable record of what actually happened, not just what the press release claimed. The built-in social post generator can draft a LinkedIn or Facebook post straight from a digest entry, tier-gated to subscribers.

**The investor's version of the problem is: what's true about this specific company, right now?**

This is where the company directory earns its keep. Every company profile carries a computed signal score built from three cross-referenced sources: article velocity over the trailing 90 days (is coverage accelerating or going quiet), the most recent detected deal signal (funding round, M&A, partnership — with LLM-extracted amount and counterparty where disclosed), and aggregated sentiment across every article mentioning that company, broken down by label and confidence, not just a single "positive/negative" badge. The directory is sortable by relevance, trending, funded, or watchlist, and exports to CSV for anyone doing their own modeling.

Two features go deeper for diligence-stage work. The framework competitive analysis engine scores a company against named peers across six weighted dimensions pulled from its actual news coverage — not a self-reported pitch deck — and renders it as a radar chart with a paragraph assessment per company. The deal signal detail page cross-references a single detected deal against that company's sentiment history, competitive score, and any open regulatory events, so a Series C or acquisition rumor doesn't have to be evaluated in isolation.

Neither audience sees the other's view. The marketer never touches the company directory. The investor never opens the trend page. But both are reading rows from the same harvested-article table, filtered through a different lens built for a different decision.

The company directory, deal signals, and market digest are all live and free to try at app.bigskylabs.ai.
