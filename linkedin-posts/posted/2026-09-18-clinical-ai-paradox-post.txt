1,357 AI medical devices have been cleared by the FDA.

3 have been tested on whether they actually help patients.

That's 99.8% with zero outcome data.

Meanwhile:
- AI detected 39% more brain aneurysms than radiologists alone (3,856 CT scans, Northwell Health — published in JACR this week)
- AI hit 67% diagnostic accuracy in Harvard ER triage vs. 50–55% for attending physicians (published in Science)
- On treatment plans: AI scored 89% vs. 34% for 46 doctors

The paradox: AI is proving itself where it's tested. The problem is almost nobody's testing.

The FDA's 510(k) pathway clears devices by "substantial equivalence" — not clinical proof. Each generation of device inherits the evidence gap of the last.

1,357 cleared. 3 tested. That's not a rounding error. That's a system design choice.
