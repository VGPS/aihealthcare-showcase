Every FHIR implementation sits on top of a regulatory layer that most teams aren't tracking in real time.

And right now, that layer is moving faster than any changelog.

In the past 12 months alone:
→ FDA cleared 100+ new AI/ML-enabled medical devices via 510(k) and De Novo pathways
→ CMS issued proposed and final rules reshaping reimbursement for AI-assisted care
→ ONC's interoperability mandates continue expanding the USCDI data set that FHIR R4/R5 implementations depend on
→ 43 state laws regulating AI in healthcare are now enacted across 24 states — adding a compliance layer on top of every federal mandate

For teams building on FHIR and OpenEHR, these aren't abstract policy questions. They're dependencies.

A CMS NCD changes what your value-based care platform can bill for.
An FDA guidance update changes what your clinical decision support tool needs to disclose.
A state AI transparency law changes what your ambient scribe must tell the patient.

I built bigskylabs.ai to track exactly this — daily monitoring of FDA 510(k) clearances, De Novo classifications, PMA approvals, CMS proposed and final rules, NCDs, and state-level AI legislation, all surfaced alongside the market intelligence (funding rounds, acquisitions, clinical trial results) that gives them context.

The goal: anyone building AI-powered health tech — on FHIR, on OpenEHR, or anywhere in the stack — should be able to answer "what changed this week that affects what I'm building?" in under two minutes.
