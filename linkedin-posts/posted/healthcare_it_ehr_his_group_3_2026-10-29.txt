Every article my platform has ever harvested is still there. None of it gets purged, archived-and-forgotten, or quietly dropped after some retention window expires. It's a repository-of-record by design — the same philosophy behind why most of you can't just delete a patient record because it's old.

The hard part wasn't the "never delete" rule — that's easy. It was doing it while staying legally clean on copyright: full-text scraping and permanent storage of someone else's article is a real exposure. The fix was a hard excerpt cap (a few hundred characters, cut at a sentence boundary, never mid-thought) plus honoring robots.txt before any page gets touched at all. You keep the fact and the provenance link forever; you don't keep the publisher's full article forever.

That's a pattern Health IT teams deal with constantly in a different shape — how do you retain what you're legally and operationally required to keep, without over-retaining what creates risk instead of value?

Curious how your teams draw that line on non-clinical data (vendor correspondence, audit logs, external content feeds) where the retention rules are less obviously spelled out than they are for PHI.
