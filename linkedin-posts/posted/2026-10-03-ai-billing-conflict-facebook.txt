Your hospital's AI and your insurance company's AI are fighting over your medical claim.

And neither one is wrong.

---

Here's what's happening:

When you're hospitalized, AI tools now scan physician notes and find every diagnosis code and severity level the documentation supports.

These codes are real — they're in the chart. But they justify higher payment.

On the other side, your insurer's AI looks at the same claim and compares it to a statistical model of what similar cases typically cost.

If the code looks like an outlier — denied.

Both systems are doing exactly what they were designed to do.

---

The numbers are getting impossible to ignore.

Blue Cross Blue Shield reported last week that hospital AI coding added $942 million to its costs over two years.

Major Medicare Advantage insurers more than doubled their denial rates for post-acute care after deploying AI review tools.

In an AMA survey, 61% of physicians said insurer AI is increasingly denying medically necessary care.

Some reported denial rates 16× normal when insurer AI first went live.

---

Four states passed laws in 2025 requiring physician review of AI-generated denials before they become final.

CMS issued rules for Medicare Advantage in 2024.

But most commercially insured Americans — through self-funded employer plans — aren't covered by those protections yet.

---

The people caught in the middle are patients.

Waiting for approvals. Appealing denials. Absorbing the overflow of two adversarial AI systems running at scale against each other.
