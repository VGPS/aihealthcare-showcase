=== FACEBOOK POST BODY ===

What happens when the AI your hospital uses to document your care conflicts with the AI your insurance company uses to review it?

The answer is now hitting the news — and there's a $942 million price tag on it.

Blue Cross Blue Shield just reported that hospitals' AI-assisted medical coding added nearly $1 billion in extra costs to its plans over two years. Hospitals say AI helps them accurately document the full complexity of care. Insurers say AI is catching inflated billing. Both sides are deploying AI to fight each other — and the battleground is your medical claim.

Here's what that looks like in practice:

When a physician documents your hospital stay, AI tools now scan those notes and identify additional diagnosis codes and severity levels that support higher reimbursement. These aren't fabricated — they're documented in the chart. But they justify higher payment.

On the insurer side, AI tools automatically flag those same higher-complexity codes as suspicious and downcode or deny the claim. Prior authorization denial rates at Humana jumped 54% after AI review tools deployed; UnitedHealthcare more than doubled its post-acute care denial rate between 2019 and 2022.

Doctors are feeling it. In a survey from the AMA, 61% of physicians said they are concerned that health insurers' use of AI is increasing medically unjustified prior authorization denials. Some reported denial rates 16 times higher than normal when insurer AI systems first went live.

At least six states — California, Arizona, Nebraska, Texas, Illinois, and Maryland — passed laws by 2025 prohibiting insurance companies from using AI alone — with no physician review — to deny medical care. More states followed in 2026. Congress and CMS have both issued guidance, but coverage for commercial insurance remains sparse.

Meanwhile, McKinsey estimates AI saves insurers $380M–$970M per $10 billion in revenue through claims management and prior auth. That "savings" is largely care that wasn't paid for. From the hospital's perspective, that's revenue denied.

The people caught in the middle are patients — waiting for approvals, appealing denials, and absorbing the administrative overflow of two adversarial AI systems running at scale against each other.
