"FDA is working on AI guidance" tells an RA team almost nothing actionable. Where that guidance actually sits — discussion paper, open comment period, draft guidance, final guidance, or active enforcement — is the difference between "watch and wait" and "this changes our submission strategy next quarter."

I track regulatory items through an explicit lifecycle stage, not just a binary tracked/not-tracked flag, across jurisdictions — U.S. FDA, EU AI Act, UK MHRA, individual U.S. states. A docket in comment period and a docket already in final guidance both show up in a news search; only one of them should be changing what you're filing this quarter.

The jurisdictions compound the complexity in a way that's easy to underweight if your team is primarily FDA-focused: an EU AI Act provision moving into enforcement can affect a submission strategy for a device that's also seeking FDA clearance, even if nothing changed on the U.S. side that week.

For the RA professionals here: how granular is your internal tracking on rulemaking stage — do you distinguish draft vs. final guidance in your own monitoring, or is "guidance exists" treated as one bucket?
