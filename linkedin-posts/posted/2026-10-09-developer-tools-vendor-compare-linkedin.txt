I compared developer tools across the four biggest AI healthcare vendors. Here's the honest breakdown.

**Amazon Health (AWS)**
Purpose-built and HIPAA-eligible out of the box — the compliance burden is Amazon's problem, not yours. Agentic architecture for multi-step clinical workflows, native EHR integration. Most credible starting point if you need to ship a HIPAA-covered app fast.

The catch: product focus is entirely administrative — scheduling, documentation, coding. No broader SDK or model marketplace. You're buying a managed solution. If your roadmap includes clinical decision support, Amazon hasn't shown that story yet.

**OpenAI for Healthcare**
Dual-track approach — ChatGPT Health for consumers, enterprise API for builders — gives OpenAI the widest surface area of the four. Real hospital partner validation means use cases are grounded. The ecosystem advantage is real: more tutorials, more integrations, faster ramp to prototype.

The gap: developer tooling details are thin for a suite this prominent. Fine-tuning endpoints for clinical use cases, structured output schemas for EHR data — none of it surfaces clearly. Consumer and enterprise lines also create positioning complexity for developers choosing an entry point.

**Google for Health**
Med-PaLM 2 underpins MedLM — foundation models fine-tuned for healthcare — and it's the strongest research pedigree of the four. The $10M clinician AI training investment signals ecosystem-building, not just model releases. Medical imaging foundations (chest X-ray, dermatology) are a genuine differentiator no other vendor matches.

The problem: MedLM is described as available to customers "exploring applications" — that's a waitlist, not a developer tool. The research lead doesn't yet translate to builder momentum.

**Claude for Healthcare (Anthropic)**
Newest entrant, positioned as complementary rather than standalone — honest framing for where it sits. What that undersells: a 200K token context window is a practical superpower for clinical document work. Discharge summaries, prior auth packets, full encounter histories — feed the whole thing. No chunking required.

The limitation: healthcare-specific API tooling is thin and there's no fine-tuning pathway. You own the infrastructure around the model.

---

My read:

No single vendor wins. Amazon owns the compliance layer. Google owns the research credibility. OpenAI owns the ecosystem. Anthropic owns the context window.

The production healthcare apps gaining traction are stitching at least two of these together — usually Amazon for HIPAA infrastructure and either Claude or GPT-4o for reasoning. The biggest gap across all four: none of them have a clear developer SDK story for clinical decision support. That's the next battleground.
