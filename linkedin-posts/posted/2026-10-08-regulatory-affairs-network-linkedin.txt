The FDA has cleared over 950 AI/ML-enabled medical devices. Most regulatory affairs teams are tracking maybe a handful of them.

That gap is a competitive intelligence problem.

Every 510(k) clearance names the applicant, the device classification, and the predicate. For an RA team preparing their own submission, that's a roadmap. Which predicates are FDA accepting for AI-assisted diagnostics? Which De Novo classifications are establishing new device types — and opening the door for 510(k) filers behind them? Where is FDA drawing the line on Predetermined Change Control Plans?

The answers are public. They're just buried.

Meanwhile the regulatory environment for AI is compounding:

→ FDA's SaMD action plan continues to evolve — draft guidance → final guidance cycles that affect every AI device in development
→ CMS coverage decisions for AI-assisted procedures are running 2-3 years behind clearances — cleared doesn't mean reimbursed
→ 43 state laws now regulate AI in healthcare, adding a compliance layer that varies by jurisdiction and patient population
→ CMS proposed and final rules are reshaping how AI-assisted care is coded, billed, and audited
